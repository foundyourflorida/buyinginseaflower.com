Hurricane preparedness for a new SeaFlower home: code, evacuation levels, generators and a plan

What the current Florida Building Code gives you, how to find your evacuation level, what a natural-gas generator setup looks like, and the checklist for a storm week.

By Trenton Miller, MBASeptember 4, 2026Updated September 3, 2026

Independent buyer’s guide, not the developer’s or any builder’s website. Figures are as published by the builders and developer on the dates noted and must be verified with them before you rely on them. The developer’s official website.

Quick answer

A new SeaFlower home is built to the current Florida Building Code, updated every three years and, by law, not weakened locally on wind resistance, with impact-rated openings or shutters, reinforced block first floors and underground utilities described in the developer's materials. Your evacuation level (A through E) is set by Manatee County by address, independent of the FEMA flood zone, and is what determines whether you are told to leave; look it up on the county's Learn Your Level map. Natural gas service through TECO makes a standby generator practical, and a transfer switch is a before-the-slab option.

What the code gives you

Roof-to-wall connections, roof deck attachment and secondary water resistance, opening protection, and wind-load design for the coastal zone. A wind-mitigation inspection documents these for your insurer and they are the reason new construction here insures for less than older homes.

Evacuation level versus flood zone

FEMA's flood zone (Zone X for the interior of SeaFlower) is an insurance map. Manatee County's evacuation levels are a surge map used for orders. A Zone X home can sit in an evacuation level. Look up your specific address before you buy, and keep the county's alert app on your phone once you move.

Generators and gas

TECO Peoples Gas serves the community. A standby generator on natural gas removes the fuel-storage problem that portable generators have. Ask the builder for a transfer-switch pre-wire and a gas stub before the slab; one of the developer's published buyer stories cites generator readiness as a reason for choosing their home.

The storm-week checklist

Confirm shutters or impact glass on every opening; photograph the home for insurance; bring in patio furniture and cart; fill the car; fill prescriptions; know the evacuation route (Cortez Road east to I-75) and the county shelter list; keep the elevation certificate, wind-mitigation form and policy numbers in cloud storage.

What happened in 2024

Hurricanes Helene and Milton hit before SeaFlower's first homes were built. Nearby Anna Maria Island and low-lying Cortez took surge damage; Zone X neighborhoods a few miles inland reported wind and debris but not water. Every home here is being built to the code that followed.

Trent’s take

I tell every buyer the same three things: know your evacuation level, buy the cheap flood policy anyway, and pre-wire for a generator before the slab because it costs a fraction of retrofitting. New construction is the best hurricane answer on this coast; it is not a reason to skip the plan.

Sources: Florida Statute 553.73; Manatee County Emergency Management evacuation levels; FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer; seaflower.com buyer stories (Jun 1, 2026); David Weekley utility listings (TECO Peoples Gas).
